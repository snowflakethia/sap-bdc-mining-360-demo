Demo Overview

Simulated data for a fictional operator on real SAP BDC IDs · Today is 22 Oct 2026 (Q2 FY2027)

SAP tells you what happened. Snowflake adds why, what next and what it costs.

The demo reads a real SAP Business Data Cloud tenant zero-copy, joins it in Snowflake with the operation's own systems, and answers three hard questions a mining executive asks. Each question is an epic: E1 for Finance, E2 for Mining Operations and E3 for Supply Chain. You will see E1, E2 and E3 throughout the app; hover one to see its name and question.

C1 (C1 cash cost) is the mining industry's name for the direct cash cost to produce one tonne of ore and put it on a ship: trucks, people, explosives, processing, power, rail and port. It excludes royalties, ocean freight and depreciation. E1 asks why it rose.

E1Finance

Where the margin went

"Why did C1 cost per tonne rise last quarter, what are the levers, and what did the operation earn?"

Asked by Chief Financial Officer, Director of Finance

The quarter closed and margin is under pressure. The CFO wants to know what the operation earned, why the cost of each tonne went up, and whether that rise was in the plan. Three pages answer this in order, from the bottom line down to the journal lines.

Why SAP alone cannot answer it

SAP holds the cost postings but not the tonnes mined, processed and shipped, so it cannot put cost or margin per tonne.

What is C1? C1 is a mining-industry cost measure (not a general accounting term) used by miners and analysts to compare mines. C1 cash cost is the direct cost to produce one tonne of ore and get it onto the ship: haul trucks and mobile fleet (diesel, tyres, maintenance), people, drill, blast and crush, rail and port, and site overheads. It excludes royalties, ocean freight and depreciation. C1 per tonne = C1 cost in the quarter / ore tonnes processed.

E2Mining Operations

Fix trucks before failure

"Which haul trucks are likely to fail in the next 14 days, what will it cost in lost tonnes, and are the spares in stock?"

Asked by General Manager Operations, Maintenance Superintendent

Finance has traced the overrun to the haul fleet. The maintenance team wants to stop failures before they happen: which trucks are at risk, what a stop costs in tonnes, and where the fleet hours go. Three pages take them from warning to cost to action.

Why SAP alone cannot answer it

SAP records a failure only when a notification is raised. The warning signs are in fleet telemetry, which SAP does not hold.

The story in three pages

The same pages also answer

Which truck should we pull into the workshop this week?How much do unplanned stops cost us in tonnes?Do autonomous trucks lose fewer hours than manned ones?

Built on 10 SAP data products (22 L0 views), 12 gold tables and 8 verified questions in the semantic view MINING_OPERATIONS.

E3Supply Chain

One supplier, whole fleet

"Which critical spares run out before a closed supplier recovers, who can replace it, and what does a port outage cost?"

Asked by Head of Supply Chain, Port and Logistics Manager

On 17 August the ship loader at Port Hedland stopped for 36 hours, and its drive motor was out of stock. Today two supplier plants are closed. The supply chain team wants to know what the outage cost, which critical spares run out before supply recovers, and who can replace a closed supplier. Three pages follow the story from the berth to the decision.

Why SAP alone cannot answer it

SAP knows suppliers and stock, but not which trucks depend on a part, how many tonnes those trucks move, or what happens at the berth.

The story in three pages

The same pages also answer

Which critical spares run out before supply recovers?Who can replace the closed wheel supplier?What does a port outage cost at quarter end?
